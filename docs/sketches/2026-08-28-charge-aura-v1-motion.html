01

Making the breath visible

±3.5% of height was about one pixel at the battle camera's framing — technically alive, practically invisible. Four changes, none of which cost a draw call:

1 · bigger, but later

Amplitude ramps in

±3.0% during the entrance rising to ±7.5% once the bounce is spent, driven by the same decay term. It never fights the bounce, and it arrives exactly where the aura would otherwise go quiet.

2 · the big one

Brightness moves with it

Height alone is a few pixels. Coupling the same factor to alpha at 1.6× gives roughly ±12% brightness in phase with the swell — and brightness is legible at any size. This is what actually makes it read as breathing.

3

The edge flutter follows

The existing top-edge displacement (DISPLACEMENT_U, today 0.055) scales with the breath instead of sitting constant, so the silhouette gets choppier on the swell and calms on the ebb. Raise its base to about 0.075 while you are there.

4

It shimmers around the ring

Per-face breath phase now comes from a smooth three-lobed function of the face angle rather than an independent hash per panel. Neighbours stay close — which is what keeps the corner steps under the mask's tolerance — while opposite sides of the wall swell visibly out of step.

Lean is measured from the ground plane, so 90° is the vertical core and 40° is the shallow outer splash. The flares are shorter, lighter, faster and more damped than the core — they flick where the core swings. Firing them 40 ms apart outward makes the burst read as a splash crown rather than three walls appearing at once.

Cost

Still two draw calls

Do not add two more MeshInstance3D children — that would be 4 instances and 4 draw calls against ceilings of 2. Append all three rings into the one ArrayMesh, one surface, 120 vertices, with the ring's identity and its per-ring constants baked into vertex colour at build time.

Instance, draw-call and node ceilings all stay exactly where they are, and the assertions in _buildOwnedLayers() keep protecting something real.

Mechanism

A flare grows by unfurling

Base and tip of a leaning ring sit on the same radial line, so a vertex only needs its base radius carried alongside it. The vertex stage then does mix(base, full, scale) — radius and height interpolate together and the ring pivots at the floor as it opens. Same single scale factor the core wall uses.

Grade

Flares are bright at the tip

The core wall is faint at its base and strong up top. The flares invert that: near-nothing where they meet the floor, brightest at their leading edge. Two reasons — a bright leading edge reads as motion, and piling three rings' density at the foot would build exactly the solid collar WALL_BOTTOM_STRENGTH exists to prevent.

Knock-on

The ground pool has to widen

The outer flare reaches r 1.16 u; today's ground plane is 1.86 u across, so it would sit outside its own light. That goes to about 2.50 u, and GROUND_SPILL_ALPHA comes down from 0.30 to roughly 0.22 because the flares now light the floor themselves.

03

The risk worth taking seriously

A 2.3 u footprint starts to look like an area effect. The design doc is explicit that a bright ring on the ground already means “this area is affected” in this project's vocabulary — danger zones, movement range — and that stating that for a single-target cast is wrong. The outer flare is the closest this effect has come to saying it.

Three things keep it on the right side: the outer ring is the faintest of the three, its arc is broken by the per-face phase rather than being a continuous circle, and it never settles into a static shape. I would hold the total footprint at 2.3 u and not push it further — and if it still reads as a zone marker in the debug scene, shorten the outer flare before dimming it, because the shape is what carries that meaning, not the brightness.

Alpha stacking at the foot. WALL_OPACITY 0.47 was chosen so two overlapping faces integrate to the 0.72 the silhouette was accepted at. Near the base the near and far halves of three rings can now stack six deep. The tip-bright grade above is the main defence; the flare opacities of 0.32 and 0.22 are the second. This wants checking in the debug scene at the harshest preset before anything else is tuned.

04

The curve

Core Mid flare Outer flare Wall alpha Breath amplitude

Each ring is the same maths with different constants, so there is one function to write and three sets of numbers to tune. The dashed muted line is the breath's own amplitude ramping from ±3% to ±7.5% as the bounces die — the handoff from entrance to idle, made explicit.

05

Decide before I build

Are two flares one too many?

Two gives a tiered splash and a silhouette that changes shape as it opens. One mid flare alone is calmer, cheaper to tune, and much further from reading as an area marker. Toggle Flares and judge against the single wall.

Outward or inward stagger?

Sketched outward — core, then mid at +40 ms, then outer at +80 ms — so the burst splashes away from the caster. Reversed, it reads as energy being pulled inward and gathered, which is arguably the better statement for a charge. Genuinely a toss-up; say which you want and it is one line.

Is ±7.5% with coupled brightness enough now?

It should be plainly visible at the real framing. If it still isn't, the next lever is the coupling ratio on brightness, not more height — more height starts fighting the silhouette the bounce establishes.

skip_to_settle() still has no still frame

Unchanged from rev 3: it should return normalized 0.60 (1.20 s), and SETTLE_NORMALIZED_TIME stops being the same value as the release start.
