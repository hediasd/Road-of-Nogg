Charge aura v2:
spin, churn, collapse

v1 grew out of the floor and settled into a quiet idle. v2 never settles: the whole stack turns around the caster, every blade churns up and down hard for the full two seconds, and at 1.70s the charge is spent rather than dimmed. This sketch exists mainly to settle that last question — three candidate release shapes, side by side, on the same clock.

The three Release buttons are the actual question. Scrub to 1.70s and step forward through each. A 2D approximation of the proposed curves, not a Godot capture — the silhouette is indicative, the timing and amplitudes are the proposal.

01

How the fade should work

An aura that has been turning and churning for 1.7 seconds has momentum. A uniform alpha ramp contradicts it — the motion is still going when the brightness admits it is over, and the last thing the eye sees is a static shape getting dimmer. The release should convert that momentum into a direction instead. Three ways to do that:

Option A

Uniform fade

What v1 does: one alpha ramp over everything. Cheapest, and it is the right answer for an effect that was already still. Here it throws away every bit of energy the previous 1.7 seconds built, and reads as a dimmer switch.

Option B · recommended

Collapse and whip

Radius contracts to ~55% and height drops to ~40% on an ease-in, while the spin accelerates 2.2×. The charge converges on the caster and is gone. It says the energy was spent — which is what a pre-cast telegraph should say, since the spell fires next.

Option C

Expand and thin

The same machinery with one sign flipped: blades fly outward and thin as they go. Reads as a burst leaving the caster. Legitimate — but it states release, and the spell's own effect is about to state that far more loudly. Two releases in a row blunt each other.

Whichever shape wins, two details matter more than the shape does, and both are in every option above.

Detail 1 · the tell

Flash before the fall

60ms at 1.35× brightness starting exactly at 1.70s, before any alpha comes off. Energy about to leave is brighter than energy idling. Without it the release has no onset — it just starts being less. This one beat is most of what sells the whole thing.

Detail 2 · never all at once

Blades go out in a wave

Each blade's extinction is offset by its own angle, so the ring unzips in the spin direction over ~140ms rather than all thirty blades vanishing together. The last thing on screen is motion, not a shape dimming. The per-face phase this needs already exists from v1.

The ground spill lags the blades by ~80ms and goes out last, so the floor keeps a residual glow for a beat after the wall is gone. A simultaneous cut on both layers reads as a dropped frame.

03

Spin and churn

Spin

A flywheel, not a turntable

Fast off the launch (~340°/s), easing back to a ~230°/s hold — roughly 1.55s per revolution — then accelerating into the release. A constant rate reads mechanical; a rate with a history reads driven.

The three rings turn at slightly different speeds (1.0 / 1.15 / 1.32×), so the stack shears instead of moving as one rigid body. That differential is most of the vortex read.

Spin

It must be set, never accumulated

Rotation is rotation.y = spinAngle(t) evaluated from the effect's own clock — never += per frame. v1's whole determinism property depends on the image being a pure function of the seek position, and an accumulated rotation is the one thing that would quietly break it.

Churn

Sustained, not decaying

v1's breath ramps up as the entrance dies and settles around ±7.5%. v2 inverts the intent: the per-blade oscillation holds at ±20% for the whole two seconds and never resolves into an idle. Faster too — the blades should visibly pump, not shimmer.

Consequence

The ring has to become blades

This is the structural change v2 forces, and it comes straight out of v1's own measurements — see below.

04

Why v2 cannot keep v1's continuous ring

v1 measured the corner gap. Faces share corner vertices, so when neighbours move independently the rim splits there. At v1's ±7.5% breath and a face mix of 0.45, the worst gap was 0.0337u — 0.8px at battle framing, comfortably hidden by the mask's ragged top edge.

v2 asks for ±20% and near-full per-blade independence. That scales the gap by roughly 2.7× on amplitude and 1.8× on independence: about 4 px of notch at every corner, on a rim the spin now drags across the screen where it cannot be missed. The mask has no side margin — v1's own measurement, alpha ~253 in its edge columns — so there is nothing to hide it behind.

The fix is not to soften the churn. It is to stop pretending the ring is continuous: make each face a discrete blade with soft side edges, and neighbours moving independently becomes the intended read rather than a tear. One line in the fragment stage does it — a smoothstep falloff on UV.x at both edges — with no new texture, and it makes the spin read better too, because you see individual blades sweep past instead of a wall rotating.

05

Decide before I build

Collapse or expand?

I recommend collapse: the spell's own effect fires immediately afterwards and will state "release" far more loudly, so a telegraph that also bursts outward competes with it. Collapse says spent, which is the one thing the spell itself cannot say. One sign flip either way.

Is 1.70s the right release, given the duration stays 2.00s?

That leaves a 0.30s window, which is enough for flash, whip, unzip and residual only because they overlap. Pulling it earlier than ~1.65s starts eating the hold; later than ~1.75s and the unzip has to compress into something that reads as a cut.

Does the spin keep a fixed direction across casts?

Sketched fixed, so it becomes a recognisable signature. Seeding the direction would make casts differ, but a telegraph benefits from being identical every time — the player should recognise it instantly, not appreciate its variety.

Do the flares survive v2 at all?

v1's verification found they read as flat terraced plates at the battle pitch. Spin may rescue them — a plate that turns is much easier to read as energy than a plate that sits — or it may make the terracing more obvious. This sketch assumes they stay. If they still fail once spinning, v2 is the moment to cut them to a single flare or none.
