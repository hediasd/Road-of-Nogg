Charge aura v2:
spin, churn, disperse

v1 grew out of the floor and settled into a quiet idle. v2 never settles: the whole stack turns around the caster, every blade churns up and down hard, and at 1.50s the charge disperses outward rather than dimming. The release shape was the open question this sketch was made to settle. It is settled — expand — and the alternative is kept below, because the rejected option is half the record.

01

The release: expand, from 1.50s

An aura that has been turning and churning for a second and a half has momentum. A uniform alpha ramp contradicts it — the motion is still going when the brightness admits it is over, and the last thing the eye sees is a static shape getting dimmer. The release converts that momentum into a direction. Three ways were considered; the outward one was chosen.

Option A

Uniform fade

What v1 does: one alpha ramp over everything. Cheapest, and it is the right answer for an effect that was already still. Here it throws away every bit of energy the previous 1.7 seconds built, and reads as a dimmer switch.

Option B · not taken

Collapse and whip

Radius contracts to ~55% and height to ~40%, spin accelerating 2.2×, the charge converging on the caster. It says the energy was spent. Kept on the toggle rather than deleted: if the expansion ever competes with the spell that follows it, this is the alternative and it is one sign flip away.

Option C · chosen

Expand and flatten

Blades fly outward to +70% radius while dropping to 55% height, spin accelerating 2.2× so they spiral rather than travel straight. The charge disperses along the floor.

The flattening is what makes this work rather than compete with the spell that follows. A tall outward burst is a second spell going off a beat before the real one; a fast, low, spreading ring is the charge coming apart. The expansion states the direction, the flatten states that it is dissipating rather than firing.

Two details matter more than the shape does, and both are in every option above.

Detail 1 · the tell

Flash before the fall

60ms at 1.35× brightness starting exactly at 1.50s, before any alpha comes off. Energy about to leave is brighter than energy idling. Without it the release has no onset — it just starts being less. This one beat is most of what sells the whole thing.

Detail 2 · never all at once

Blades go out in a wave

Each blade's extinction is offset by its own angle, so the ring unzips in the spin direction over ~140ms rather than all thirty blades vanishing together. The last thing on screen is motion, not a shape dimming. The per-face phase this needs already exists from v1.

Moving the release from 1.70s to 1.50s buys 200ms, and it is spent on travel rather than on a slower fade: the blades need distance for the outward direction to read at all, while each blade's own extinction stays a fast 220ms. The ground spill lags by 180ms and goes out last, so the floor keeps a residual glow after the blades are gone. A simultaneous cut on both layers reads as a dropped frame.

02

Could the release come at 1.20s?

Only as part of a proportional tightening. On its own it does not move the release earlier so much as delete the hold — and the hold is the frame the camera is there for.

Hold is measured from where the core's bounce amplitude falls under 3% — the first frame that reads as charged rather than still arriving. Blades are how many of the ten sweep past a fixed point during that hold, which is what actually makes the spin legible.

Why 1.20 alone fails

The entrance is still settling

The core's bounce is not spent until 1.11s. Releasing at 1.20 leaves a 0.09s hold — five frames — so the aura goes from arriving straight to leaving and is never simply there.

The spin suffers worse: 312° means it never completes a revolution, and only 0.6 blades pass a fixed point during the hold. Something that turns less than once does not read as spinning; it reads as having swung.

What makes it work

Compress the entrance, speed the spin

Scale the entrance to 0.80× in time (rise 0.16→0.13s, periods and decays with it) and the spin to 1.24×. The bounce is then spent by 0.89s, the hold comes back to 0.31s, and the spin completes 387° with 2.5 blades passing — matching what 1.50 gives today.

Toggle Tighten with release at 1.20 to see it.

The consequence to accept

The whole effect ends at 1.70s

The release window should stay 0.50s rather than stretching to fill a fixed 2.00s runtime — a 0.80s dispersal is 40% of the effect spent fading, and it goes limp. So releasing at 1.20 means the aura is over at 1.70.

That is a real change to the brief, which asked for about two seconds. If a snappier telegraph is wanted, this is the version of it; if two seconds is fixed, 1.50 already is the answer.

04

Spin and churn

Spin

A flywheel, not a turntable

Fast off the launch (~340°/s), easing back to a ~230°/s hold — roughly 1.55s per revolution — then accelerating into the release. A constant rate reads mechanical; a rate with a history reads driven.

The three rings turn at slightly different speeds (1.0 / 1.15 / 1.32×), so the stack shears instead of moving as one rigid body. That differential is most of the vortex read.

Spin

It must be set, never accumulated

Rotation is rotation.y = spinAngle(t) evaluated from the effect's own clock — never += per frame. v1's whole determinism property depends on the image being a pure function of the seek position, and an accumulated rotation is the one thing that would quietly break it.

Churn

Sustained, not decaying

v1's breath ramps up as the entrance dies and settles around ±7.5%. v2 inverts the intent: the per-blade oscillation holds at ±20% for the whole two seconds and never resolves into an idle. Faster too — the blades should visibly pump, not shimmer.

Consequence

The ring has to become blades

This is the structural change v2 forces, and it comes straight out of v1's own measurements — see below.

05

Why v2 cannot keep v1's continuous ring

v1 measured the corner gap. Faces share corner vertices, so when neighbours move independently the rim splits there. At v1's ±7.5% breath and a face mix of 0.45, the worst gap was 0.0337u — 0.8px at battle framing, comfortably hidden by the mask's ragged top edge.

v2 asks for ±20% and near-full per-blade independence. That scales the gap by roughly 2.7× on amplitude and 1.8× on independence: about 4 px of notch at every corner, on a rim the spin now drags across the screen where it cannot be missed. The mask has no side margin — v1's own measurement, alpha ~253 in its edge columns — so there is nothing to hide it behind.

The fix is not to soften the churn. It is to stop pretending the ring is continuous: make each face a discrete blade with soft side edges, and neighbours moving independently becomes the intended read rather than a tear. One line in the fragment stage does it — a smoothstep falloff on UV.x at both edges — with no new texture, and it makes the spin read better too, because you see individual blades sweep past instead of a wall rotating.

06

Settled, and still open

Settled 2026-08-29. Expand, not collapse — with the flatten carrying the difference between dispersing and firing. Release moves from 1.70s to 1.50s, spending the extra 200ms on outward travel rather than on a slower fade. Spin keeps a fixed direction across every cast, so the telegraph is instantly recognisable rather than varied. The flares stay for now, despite v1's verification finding that they read as terraced plates at the battle pitch.

Does the spin rescue the flares?

The one thing v2 might fix for free. A plate that sits still reads as geometry; a plate that turns has a much better chance of reading as energy. If they still fail once spinning, cutting to a single flare or none is a one-constant change — but that judgement needs the effect actually running, so it belongs at V2-F rather than now.

Does the release compete with the spell that follows?

The reason collapse was the earlier recommendation. The flatten is the mitigation, and it should be judged against a real cast rather than in isolation. Collapse stays on the toggle and in the constants precisely so this stays cheap to reverse.

Does +70% radius reawaken the area-marker read?

v1 held the resting footprint at 2.3u for this reason. The expansion is transient and moving, which is the opposite of what a zone marker does, but it is still the widest this effect has ever been. Worth a look at V2-F before it is called done.
