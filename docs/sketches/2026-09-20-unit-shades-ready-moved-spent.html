Ready, moved, spent

What this settled. That a unit on the hex board carries three shades, not two, and that the two darkened ones are chosen in perceived lightness rather than in the shader's multiplier. Moved — walked but not yet committed to an action, still the player's to command — sits about a quarter of the way to black. Spent — turn over, only read-only inspection left — sits about three fifths of the way. Both are one multiply on the unit's own albedo, so every hue survives and every part of the model darkens by the same factor.

The correction this page exists for. unit_darken multiplies ALBEDO, which is linear light, and the frame is then gamma-encoded. A multiplier of 0.72 therefore does not remove 72% of the brightness a player sees — it removes 44%. Picking the pair by eye in multiplier space gave two shades that were far milder on screen than their numbers suggested, and the mild one was nearly invisible. The sliders below are in perceived terms and print the shader constant they imply.

What was rejected. Replacing the unit with its own luminance under a slate tint — the treatment that shipped first. It made every unit the same grey, so a spent unit lost both its team and its element, and it was applied by reading the drawn frame back through a slightly inflated hull, which broke up at the retro pipeline's internal resolution. It is reconstructed in the last panel from its own formula, beside the one that replaced it.

Why these two numbers

The multiplier and what the player sees are not the same curve. The dashed line is what a multiplier looks like it should do; the solid line is what it does once the frame is gamma-encoded. The two marks are the chosen shades.

Screen is the share of the unit's own brightness a player still sees. Linear is the share of its albedo. The constant is what the adapter hands the shader — MOVED_DARKEN and SPENT_DARKEN in HexBattleVisualAdapter.

Against the treatment it replaced

Thunder and ice, the two units the old treatment hurt most: a yellow unit went olive, and a pale blue one went the same grey as everything else.

Shades are applied by playback, not by the simulation — a unit darkens when the screen has shown the move or the action that earned it, never when the simulator decided it. The rule and the two constants live in src/presentation/battle/HexBattleVisualAdapter.gd; the multiply itself is unit_darken in assets/shaders/retro_surface.gdshader.
