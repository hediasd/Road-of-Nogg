# Aspect 18: Grid Mechanics & Large Monsters

A comparative breakdown of how tactical RPGs handle multi-tile unit footprints, massive boss encounters, and grid spatial occupancy.

---

## 1. Unit Grid Occupancy

Rules dictating how many tiles a single unit occupies and how it affects movement and targeting.

### Approach 1.1: Standard 1x1 Uniform Grid
Every unit, regardless of lore size, occupies exactly 1 tile. Large bosses are either scaled down visually or act as stationary background elements with multiple targetable 1x1 nodes.
* **Games Following This Approach:** *Fire Emblem Series, Final Fantasy Tactics, Tactics Ogre: Let Us Cling Together / Reborn, Into the Breach*
* **Detailed Variations:**
  - *Final Fantasy Tactics:* Even massive dragons occupy 1 tile, though their sprites heavily overlap adjacent tiles.
  - *Into the Breach:* Giant Vek bugs still only occupy a single square on the 8x8 grid.

### Approach 1.2: Multi-Tile Entities (2x2, 3x3, etc.)
Large monsters explicitly occupy multiple tiles on the grid, blocking movement across a wider area and allowing them to be targeted from more angles or hit by multiple AoE overlapping tiles.
* **Games Following This Approach:** *Popolocrois Series, Dofus & Wakfu, Mario + Rabbids Kingdom Battle, Disgaea Series*
* **Detailed Variations:**
  - **Popolocrois Series:** Features 2x2 and sometimes larger bosses on the tactical battle grid. Their large size means standard AoE attacks can hit them easily, and they naturally body-block entire chokepoints.
  - *Dofus & Wakfu:* Certain massive bosses or summons take up multiple tiles, altering line-of-sight and locking mechanics for melee attackers.
  - *Disgaea Series:* Later entries introduced "Giant Monsters" (via Magichange or natural size) which occupy significantly more grid space and have massively expanded attack ranges.

### Approach 1.3: Multi-Part Boss Entities (Segmented Targeting)
The boss occupies a massive portion of the grid, but is broken down into multiple targetable segments (Head, Left Arm, Right Arm, Core), each acting as a distinct 1x1 or 2x2 entity.
* **Games Following This Approach:** *Vagrant Story, Shining Force Series, Grandia Series*
* **Detailed Variations:**
  - *Shining Force Series:* Notable bosses (like the Kraken or Dark Dragon) have multiple targetable parts on the map that take turns independently.
  - *Vagrant Story:* While not grid-based, massive bosses have distinct targetable limbs with their own HP and affinities, mimicking spatial segmentation.

---

## 2. Advantages & Quirks of Large Monsters

### 2.1: Body Blocking & ZOC (Zone of Control)
A 2x2 monster essentially acts as a moving wall. In games with ZOC mechanics, navigating around them becomes a significant tactical hurdle.

### 2.2: AoE Vulnerability
In many systems, if an Area-of-Effect (AoE) spell hits multiple tiles occupied by a large monster, it might take damage multiple times (once per tile hit). This turns large size into a defensive liability unless hardcoded against.

### 2.3: Attack Range Dominance
Larger monsters often naturally possess longer melee reach (e.g., striking 2 tiles away as "melee") simply because their sprite occupies more real estate.
